Checking a Notice Deadline

Practice identifying the date that matters before giving advice about a housing notice.

0% through this course
§ 1 The scenario

A tenant calls after finding a notice taped to their apartment door. They are worried that they have already missed the deadline to respond.

Before giving next-step advice, the advocate needs to identify the date on the notice, the date the tenant received it, and the type of deadline the notice creates.

§ 2 The rule

Start with the document itself. Confirm what kind of notice it is, when it was served, and whether the deadline is counted in calendar days, business days, or by a court rule.

Key points
  1. Identify the notice type - different notices can trigger different deadlines.
  2. Confirm the service date - do not rely only on when the client first saw it.
  3. Check the counting rule - weekends and holidays may matter.
§ 4 Try it

One question. You'll see the answer once you choose.

Scenario

The client says the notice was dated Monday, but they found it on Wednesday night. What should the advocate check first?

The answer: Start by confirming the notice type, service facts, and counting rule. The safest advice depends on all three, and the wrong assumption can make a short deadline even shorter.
§ 5 If you remember nothing else
  • Start with the document - the notice type drives the analysis.
  • Ask about service - how and when the notice arrived can matter.
  • Verify the counting rule - do not improvise deadlines from memory.
