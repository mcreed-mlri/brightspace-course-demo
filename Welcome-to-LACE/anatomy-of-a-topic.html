How a topic works

Every topic in LACE — including this one — is built to the same plan. Once you can see the plan, you'll always know where to look. Here it is, using the page you're reading right now as the example.

§ 1 The moment

The worst kind of training makes you hunt: where's the actual rule? Is there a quiz? How long is this going to take? Every page is laid out differently, so every page costs you a little energy just to get oriented.

LACE removes that cost by making every topic the same shape. You learn the shape once — here — and then it's invisible.

§ 2 The parts of a topic

Look at the top of this page. Every topic opens the same way and moves through the same numbered sections:

What you'll always find
  1. The eyebrow — the small line up top: which topic of how many, the kind, and the estimated minutes.
  2. The standfirst — one short paragraph telling you why this topic matters before you commit.
  3. Numbered sections (§) — the content, in a fixed order. The same idea always lives in the same place.
  4. A Try-it — one question near the end. (The next topic is all about these.)
  5. Remember — the two or three things that must stick.

Moving around is just as predictable. Use Next and Previous in the bar at the top to step through a course. The menu in that same bar opens the full outline so you can jump to any topic.

§ 3 Two ways to read

The top bar has a Read · Practice toggle. It doesn't change the content — only the pace:

Same topic, two speeds
  1. Read — the whole topic on one page. Best for skimming or coming back for reference.
  2. Practice — one section at a time with Back / Continue. Best for focus, when you want it to stick.

It's not a quiz — just a pace — and LACE remembers your choice for next time.

§ 4 Try it

One question. You'll see the answer once you choose.

Quick check

You're three topics into a course and want to re-read the key rule from topic one. What's the fastest way there?

The answer: the menu in the top bar opens the whole outline, so you can jump to any topic in one move. Previous/Next are for stepping one at a time — handy, but slower for jumps.
§ 5 If you remember nothing else
  • Every topic has the same shape — eyebrow, standfirst, numbered sections, Try-it, Remember.
  • Next / Previous step through; the menu jumps anywhere.
  • Read · Practice switches between one page and one-section-at-a-time.
