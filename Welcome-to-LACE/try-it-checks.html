The Try-it self-checks

§ 1 The moment

We've all read a page, nodded along, and realized at the bottom we couldn't say what it actually told us. Re-reading feels like learning, but it mostly builds confidence, not memory.

The fix is small and a little uncomfortable: stop and try to recall before you've got it cold. That's the whole job of the Try-it.

§ 2 What a Try-it is — and isn't

Every topic ends with one question. You pick an answer, and the explanation appears right away — whether you were right or not. That's it.

The ground rules
  1. It's not graded. No score is recorded, and a wrong pick doesn't count against you anywhere.
  2. The answer always shows. The point isn't to test you — it's to make the idea stick by having you reach for it.
  3. Guessing is fine. Committing to an answer, even a wrong one, helps you remember the right one better than skipping ahead.
  4. One per topic. Just enough to anchor the idea, never so much it feels like an exam.

So when you reach the Try-it below, don't skim past it. Actually choose. That five seconds of effort is what turns "I read it" into "I know it."

§ 3 Try it

One question. You'll see the answer once you choose.

Quick check

You're not totally sure which answer is right on a Try-it. What should you do?

The answer: just commit to your best guess. Nothing is graded, the explanation appears either way, and the act of choosing is exactly what helps the idea stick.
§ 4 If you remember nothing else
  • Try-its are not graded — they're for recall, not judgment.
  • The answer always appears when you choose, right or wrong.
  • Always commit to an answer — the reaching is the learning.
