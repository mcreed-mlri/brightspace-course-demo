Faculty content handbook

Interactive Elements

This handbook helps you recommend interactive elements while you draft or revise course content. You do not build anything in Brightspace yourself — you decide what a section needs, note it in your draft, and hand it to the LACE builder.

Use it in this order: decide whether a section should be interactive at all, choose the element that fits the learning purpose, then copy its handoff template into your draft.

How a request looks

Write your content in plain language, then mark where an element belongs with a bracket tag. No HTML, no Brightspace steps.

Step 1 · Deciding

Should this be interactive?

Interactivity should earn its place. Before you add an element, check what you actually want the learner to do with the content.

Use an element when learners need to

  • +Compare two or more things
  • +Recall a term or definition
  • +Sort examples into categories
  • +Sequence steps in order
  • +Check their own understanding
  • +Notice an important exception
  • +Explore optional detail

Use plain text when learners simply need to

  • –Read a required explanation
  • –Follow a short instruction
  • –Understand one main idea
  • –Move ahead without extra clicks
Not every page needs an interactive element. If the content is already clear, plain text may be the strongest choice.

Step 3 · Requesting

How to tag content

In your draft, mark where you want an element by writing its bracket tag right in the text, followed by the content the builder should place there. Each element below includes a handoff template you can copy.

[ACCORDION] [TABS] [CALLOUT] [QUOTE] [FLIPCARDS] [SORTING] [SEQUENCE] [FILLBLANK] [DAY ONE]

Tags are recommendations, not final build requirements. If another format fits the content better, the builder may adjust the element — so focus on the learning purpose, and let the tag express your intent.

Available now · Layout & emphasis

Accordion

Stacked headers that expand on click, so optional or supporting detail stays available without crowding the page.

[ACCORDION]

Best for

FAQs, optional details, supporting definitions, or extra examples.

Avoid when

The information is required or must be read in order.

Faculty provides

A heading or question for each item, plus the answer text.

Effort

Low

Example · try it

What if the client missed the answer deadline?{{ accChev0 }}
Move quickly: a late answer can often still be filed, but the risk of a default judgment rises by the day. Confirm the court's practice, prepare a motion to remove any default, and document the reason for the delay.
Which deadlines are calendar days vs. court days?{{ accChev1 }}
Always confirm before advising. Some clocks run on calendar days including weekends and holidays; others count only business or court days. When in doubt, calculate both and act on the earlier date.
Optional reading: the history of summary process{{ accChev2 }}
Summary process was designed as a fast, streamlined court procedure. Understanding why it moves quickly helps advocates anticipate the compressed timeline their clients face — tuck background like this into an accordion so it's there for the curious without overwhelming everyone.

Handoff template

[ACCORDION: What if the client missed the answer deadline?]
Explain the options, risks, and next steps here.
[/ACCORDION]

Tabs

Switch between related content in place — ideal for comparing two or more options, such as notice types or procedural stages.

[TABS]

Best for

Side-by-side comparisons: 14-day vs. 30-day notice, before court vs. day of court.

Avoid when

Learners need to see everything at once, or the items don't really compare.

Faculty provides

A short label and the content for each tab.

Effort

Low

Example · try it

Nonpayment of rent

Used when the tenant is behind on rent. The 14-day clock starts when proper notice is served — and the tenant may have a right to cure by paying what's owed.

No-fault termination

Used to end a tenancy at will without alleging fault. The 30-day (or full rental-period) notice must be timed correctly — errors in timing are a common, winnable defense.

Handoff template

[TABS: Compare notice types]
[TAB: 14-day notice] Use for nonpayment cases.
[TAB: 30-day notice] Use for no-fault termination.
[/TABS]

Callouts

Lift a deadline, warning, exception, or practice tip out of the flow so it can't be missed. Four tones to match the message.

[CALLOUT]

Best for

Deadlines, warnings, exceptions, reminders, key rules, and practice tips.

Avoid when

It's ordinary body text. Overusing callouts weakens the ones that matter.

Faculty provides

The tone (warning, tip, key rule, or deadline) and one or two sentences.

Effort

Low

Example · four tones

⚠ Warning

Never advise a client to ignore a court date. A missed hearing can result in an immediate default.

✎ Practice tip

Bring two copies of every document to court — one for the clerk, one for the client's file.

§ Key rule

Service must be complete and proper before the case can proceed. Defects in service are a real defense.

⏱ Deadline

The answer is generally due the Monday before the hearing date. Calculate it the moment you get the case.

Handoff template

[CALLOUT DEADLINE: The answer is generally due the Monday before the hearing date.]

Tones: [CALLOUT WARNING] · [CALLOUT TIP] · [CALLOUT KEY RULE] · [CALLOUT DEADLINE]

[QUOTE]

Best for

Short expert language, guiding principles, statutes, or client-centered reminders.

Avoid when

The passage is long, or the content is a required procedural step.

Faculty provides

The quote text and its source or attribution.

Effort

Low

Flip cards

Digital flashcards for quick recall — see the term, guess the meaning, then click to check.

[FLIPCARDS]

Best for

Quick recall of terms, definitions, or concepts — e.g. notice to quit, summary process, the answer.

Avoid when

The explanation is long or the rule is complicated.

Faculty provides

A term for the front and a short definition for the back of each card.

Effort

Medium

Example · click a card to flip →

Notice to quit Flip
A written notice that ends the tenancy and starts the clock for the next step.
Summary process Flip
The formal court procedure used to evict a tenant in Massachusetts.
The answer Flip
The tenant's written response to the complaint, raising any defenses.

Handoff template

[FLIPCARDS]
Notice to quit :: A written notice that ends the tenancy and starts the clock.
Summary process :: The formal court procedure used to evict a tenant.
[/FLIPCARDS]

Sorting gallery

Learners classify examples into categories. Tap each scenario to file it under the right notice type.

[SORTING]

Best for

Categorizing examples — e.g. proper notice / defective notice / needs review.

Avoid when

There is no clearly correct category for each item.

Faculty provides

The category names and each item paired with its correct category.

Effort

Medium

Example · tap to sort

Sequencing

Learners put steps in the right order. Best for procedures that must run in a fixed sequence.

[SEQUENCE]

Best for

Steps that must happen in order — confirm deadline, check service, save copy, review form.

Avoid when

The steps can reasonably happen in more than one order.

Faculty provides

The steps written out in their correct order.

Effort

Medium

Fill in the blanks

Learners complete a sentence from memory — a low-stakes check on a key term or exact wording.

[FILLBLANK]

Best for

Key terms, phrases, or a short recall check on exact wording.

Avoid when

Several answers could reasonably be correct — unless you list the accepted alternatives.

Faculty provides

The sentence with blanks marked, plus the accepted answer(s).

Effort

Medium

Example · type your answers

A notice to quit usually states the for termination and the for the next step.
✓ Exactly right — reason & timeline.

Handoff template

[FILLBLANK: A notice to quit usually states the ___ for termination and the ___ for the next step.]
Answers: reason ; timeline (accept: deadline)
[/FILLBLANK]
Available now · Full-page

Day One Experience

A full-page course or module opener that sets expectations, explains how to proceed, and orients learners before they begin.

[DAY ONE]

Best for

The beginning of a course or module, to set expectations and orient learners.

Avoid when

It's normal lesson content in the middle of a module.

Faculty provides

A welcome message, what to expect, how to proceed, and the first step.

Effort

High

Handoff template

[DAY ONE]
Welcome: One or two sentences that greet the learner.
What to expect: Length, topics, any required activities.
How to proceed: Where to start and what to do first.
[/DAY ONE]
